Braindance

Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 26, 2026

Braindance is an open-source app for finding games to play, on Android, iOS and the web. This page explains what information it handles, why, where that information goes, and how long it stays. If anything is unclear, email me at support@braindancegg.com.

Who I am

I'm Vladislav Kochetov, and I build and run Braindance on my own. There's no company or team behind it, so wherever this page says "I", that's me. I'm the person responsible for your information (the "data controller", in legal terms), and every email to support@braindancegg.com comes to me.

The short version

  • You can browse games without an account.
  • Braindance has no ads. I don't sell your information or share it for advertising, and I don't track you across other apps or websites.
  • If you sign in, Braindance keeps your name, profile picture, email address and the devices you're signed in on, so it can sign you in and show your account. You can delete your account at any time.

Browsing without an account

To show games, the apps ask the Braindance server for them. Like any website, the server sees your IP address and the request, such as which game you opened.

  • Your IP address is used to send the response and to limit how fast one device can send requests, which protects the service from abuse. It isn't stored with your requests.
  • My hosting provider keeps technical request logs, such as the time, the address requested and the browser or app version, for up to 30 days, so I can run the service and fix problems. These logs don't contain your IP address.
  • Game information comes from IGDB and giveaways from GamerPower. The Braindance server asks them for that data itself, so they don't receive your information from Braindance.

Signing in

Sign-in is being added to the apps. You'll sign in with Google or Apple, so Braindance never sees your password. When you sign in, Braindance receives and stores:

  • Your account ID at Google or Apple, which tells Braindance it's you next time.
  • Your email address and whether the provider has verified it. If you use Apple's Hide My Email, Braindance gets a private relay address instead of your own.
  • Your name and profile picture, if the provider shares them. Apple shares your name only the first time you sign in.

For each device or browser you sign in on, Braindance keeps a sign-in session: the device name your app reports or your browser's name and version, an approximate network location (only the first part of your IP address, which identifies a network rather than a device), and when the session was created and last used. You can see and end these sessions from your account. Braindance also keeps a short security log of sign-ins, sign-outs and account changes, with the same approximate network and device details, to protect your account.

I use this information only to sign you in, show your account and keep it secure. Braindance never links two sign-in accounts just because they share an email address. You can link them yourself while signed in.

On the web, Braindance keeps you signed in with a secure cookie that only the Braindance server can read. The web app also remembers which screen you were on in your browser's storage, which never leaves your device.

Two-step verification and passkeys

Once sign-in is available, you'll be able to protect your account with a second step when you sign in:

  • An authenticator app. Braindance stores the app's secret key, encrypted, and the time of the last code you used, so a code can't be used twice. It also stores your one-time recovery codes, but only in a scrambled form that can't be turned back into the codes.
  • Passkeys. Your fingerprint, face or device PIN never leaves your device. Braindance stores only the passkey's public key and ID, the name you give it, the kind of connection it uses, whether it's backed up to your password manager, and when it was added and last used.

If someone enters a wrong code 10 times, Braindance pauses the code step for 15 minutes and sends a security email to the verified addresses on your account. An email service delivers it from security@notify.braindancegg.com, and it never contains codes or links. Wrong codes are counted for up to 15 minutes, and the count isn't stored with your account ID in readable form.

Crash reports

If the Android or iOS app crashes, it sends a crash report to Firebase Crashlytics, a Google service, so I can find and fix the problem. A report includes what went wrong in the app's code, your device model, operating system version, the app version, and a random identifier for the app installation. It doesn't include your account or what you were browsing. Crash reports are kept for the limited time Firebase sets for them.

Trailers

Game trailers play in YouTube's embedded player. When you play one, YouTube, a Google service, receives information about your device and network under Google's Privacy Policy. Braindance uses YouTube's privacy-enhanced mode, so YouTube doesn't store cookies before you play a trailer.

This website

braindancegg.com is hosted on GitHub Pages and loads its fonts from Google Fonts, so GitHub and Google receive your IP address when you visit. The web app at app.braindancegg.com and the Braindance server are delivered through a delivery network that protects them from attacks and also processes your IP address. There are no analytics or advertising cookies on these sites.

Services I use

Braindance runs on a few outside services: a cloud server and database, short-lived storage for sign-in checks and rate limits, delivery and protection of the website and server, security emails, sign-in with Google or Apple, and crash reports. They process your information only to provide their service to me, and most of them run in the United States. The list of services I use names each one and what it does.

I don't sell your information or share it with anyone else, unless the law requires it.

How long information is kept

  • Your account, sign-in accounts, sessions and security log are kept until you delete your account. A session stops working after 30 days without use, and at most 60 days after you sign in.
  • Request logs are kept for up to 30 days.
  • Sign-in checks and rate limits expire within minutes, and counts of wrong codes within 15 minutes.
  • Your authenticator app's key, recovery codes and passkeys are kept until you remove them or delete your account.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account in the app once sign-in is available there, or at any time by emailing me at support@braindancegg.com from the address on your account. Deleting it immediately removes your name, profile picture, email addresses, linked sign-in accounts, sessions, authenticator app key, recovery codes and passkeys, and signs you out everywhere. All that's left is an anonymous record that an account existed and when security events happened, without any network or device details. If you sign in again later, you start a new account.

Your choices and rights

You can ask me to show, correct, export or delete the information Braindance has about you by emailing support@braindancegg.com. Depending on where you live, you may have more rights under laws such as the GDPR or the CCPA, including the right to complain to your data protection authority. I'll reply within 30 days.

To keep your information safe, Braindance uses encrypted connections and stores sign-in tokens only in a form that can't be turned back into the token. I'm the only person with access to the accounts that run Braindance.

Children and mature games

Braindance follows the same age rule as Twitch, which runs IGDB, where its game information comes from: if you're under 13, please don't use Braindance or create an account. I don't knowingly collect information from children under 13. If you think a child has created an account, email me at support@braindancegg.com and I'll delete it.

Like IGDB, Braindance shows games of every age rating, including mature games with violence, gore or some nudity. Games made for adults (games made around sexual content, and games rated Adults Only) are hidden from search, lists and similar games. If you open one from a link, its page shows an Adult tag and hides its images and description.

Changes to this page

When this page changes, the date at the top changes too. If a change affects how your information is used, I'll announce it here before it takes effect.
